UX/UI case study · Concept work

DB Navigator

An unsolicited redesign of Deutsche Bahn’s journey planner

DB Navigator is Deutsche Bahn’s app for planning routes, buying tickets and finding the way on trains and public transport. In this independent case study I explored how its booking could become clearer, more predictable and easier to use. The research and testing are done; the redesign is in progress.

01 · The problem

Delays, tight transfers and no clear hints

During real trips, people regularly face uncertainty: delayed trains, minimal transfer times and a lack of clear guidance. I turned this into four hypotheses about DB Navigator, and they became the basis for usability testing.

Four hypotheses, all confirmed in testing

  1. H1Confirmed

    The app doesn’t offer an alternative route when a train is delayed or a transfer is missed.

    In testingOne participant saw a delay as a threat to the whole route and didn’t know what to do next; the other got no alternative from the app and changed the route himself.

  2. H2Confirmed

    The app doesn’t draw attention to critically short transfers.

    In testingA three-minute change at an unfamiliar station made one participant doubt she could make it; the other was unsure about the transfers from the start.

  3. H3Confirmed

    Route filters are inconvenient and illogically built.

    In testingWhen the date changed, the time stayed as it was. Neither participant was warned, and fixing it meant going back to the filters.

  4. H4Confirmed

    Train labels are unclear and don’t help people find their way along the route.

    In testingNeither could tell the trains apart, or whether the next leg was a train or a bus.

02 · Competitive analysis

Search, list and details in three apps

I compared route search, the route list and the route details in FlixBus, Trainline and Ukrzaliznytsia. Each is simpler to start with. DB Navigator gives more detail and control, but it is harder to read.

01 / 03

FlixBus / FlixTrain

Route search
A quick, clear start without extra steps. Simpler than DB, but less flexible for complex trips.
Route list
The cards are easy to read and what matters is visible at once. Transfer information is less visible than in DB.
Route details
The route is laid out in one line and reads without effort, with less depth and control of detail than DB Navigator.

Ukrzaliznytsia

Route details

03 · Usability testing

What testing revealed

Two participants planned a real trip in DB Navigator: journeys with transfers, changes to the search settings and replanning after delays. Their actions and comments showed where the route, the settings or the next step were hard to understand.

Why usability testing

It shows how people actually plan a route, not what they assume or say in theory. Problems surface at the moment they happen, without the distortion of memory.

It captured
  • how people build a route
  • where they run into difficulties
  • where they start to doubt their choice

The task

Translated from Russian

A real long-distance trip with several route options and possible transfers: people go by travel time, train types and how convenient the route is.

Four findings, with the recordings

Recording · Participant 1

Participant 1 · Maria

Short transfers create uncertainty

What she did
Planned a route with transfers and judged the transfer times and how reliable the trip was.
Where it got hard
Short transfers between different routes, and not knowing what a delay meant for the trip.

  • “It always shows a three-minute transfer. How am I supposed to make it if I don’t know the station?”

  • “So many transfers scare me.”

  • “It’s a stressful situation.”

Confirms H1
She took a delay as a threat to the whole route and didn’t know what she could do next or how reliable the route still was.
Confirms H2
She doubted she could physically make the transfer; not knowing the station made her anxious even when the time was enough on paper.
Recording · Participant 1

Participant 1 · Maria

Filters and labels lack clarity

What she did
Changed the date and time of the trip, corrected the route after a mistake and compared the trains.
Where it got hard
A filter error when changing the date, the logic of the filters, and how the trains differ.

  • “I missed the filters in the first search.”

  • “Turns out you can even mark that you’re taking a bike.”

  • “It’s not always clear to me what kind of transport I’ll be on.”

Confirms H3
She made a mistake when changing the settings. The app didn’t warn her that the date and time didn’t fit, and fixing it meant going back and setting everything up again.
Confirms H4
She couldn’t tell the train types apart: the labels didn’t say what kind of transport it was and didn’t help her decide.
Recording · Participant 2

Participant 2 · Daniel

Changing plans takes extra steps

What he did
Changed the trip date in the filters, planned a route with transfers and went back to the filters to fix a mistake.
Where it got hard
After the date changed, the time stayed at the current one, so the route was wrong and he had to go back to the settings by hand.

  • “Trains are never on time, especially when you live in Hachenburg.”

Confirms H1
The app offered him no alternative: to continue the trip, he changed the route himself.
Confirms H2
He was unsure about the route with transfers from the start.
Recording · Participant 2

Participant 2 · Daniel

Delays make the next step unclear

What he did
Left the route when the train was delayed, built a new one from where he was, and did this again at every delay.
Where it got hard
No way to tell whether the route was still valid, when he would arrive, or how long the delay might be.

  • “I’m never sure I’ll get there on time.”

  • “It would help to know at least a rough time window.”

Confirms H3
The filters let him make a mistake when changing the date, with no warning.
Confirms H4
At a change of transport it wasn’t always clear whether to wait for a train or walk to a bus; the change was shown unclearly.

What testing showed

People plan trips with uncertainty and anxiety, most of all around delays, short transfers and changing route conditions.

  • A minimal transfer time without hints feels like an unsafe choice.

  • Without clear emphasis, people trust the route less.

  • Labels for a change of transport don’t tell people what to do next.

04 · Users

Who I designed for

A customer profile, and a journey map of the same trip from opening the app to the destination: what people do, feel and need at each stage.

Customer profile

Portrait generated with AI

Pain points

  • Short transfers make me anxious
  • With delays, the route quickly goes out of date
  • When I change transport, it isn’t always clear whether to wait for a train or walk to a bus
  • The date and time filters easily lead to mistakes

Needs

  • To understand whether I’ll make it there in time
  • To see the risks of a transfer in advance
  • To know what to do when a train is delayed
  • A guide to the arrival time, even a rough one

Goals

  • Travel to my family at weekends without worrying
  • Plan trips to events without constant stress
  • Feel in control of the route, even with delays
  • Know roughly when I’ll arrive instead of guessing until the last moment

06 · Redesign

The redesign, in progress

The research and testing are done; the redesign is not. Three screens are redesigned so far, each answering a problem from testing. None of them has been tested yet.

  • 3screens redesigned so far: search, the route list and connection details
  • In progressNot tested yet. Next comes a prototype test with travellers.
  • Trip filtersdate and time in one scenario, extra settings in their own block
  • Route badgesCheapest, Fewest transfers, Fastest, and they combine
  • Transfer typein the timeline, for example Bus → Train

01

Search and trip filters

What changed
I rebuilt the logic of the date and time filters into one scenario. When the date changes, the time no longer stays wrong, and the extra settings moved into a separate Trip filters block.
What it should change
People reach the right route faster, go back to the filters less often and understand the screen better, with fewer errors and less to think about.
From testing
When the date changed, the time stayed and had to be entered again.
Before · Route list
After · Route list
02

Route list

What changed
Route categories right in the results: Cheapest, Fewest transfers and Fastest. They show as badges on the routes and can be combined, so the main advantage of each option is clear without opening the filters.
What it should change
Routes are quicker to compare and their differences clear without sorting through every parameter by hand; choosing is easier and needs fewer trips back to the filters.
From testing
It was unclear how the trains differ and how reliable each option is.
Before · Connection details
After · Connection details
03

Connection details

What changed
A clear hint about the type of each transfer in the timeline, for example Bus → Train, and a warning about a tight transfer with a link to alternative routes.
What it should change
Travellers know in advance what waits at each transfer, feel less stress and judge the risks better. The route becomes more transparent, the app easier to trust, and surprises on the way less likely.
From testing
Transfers felt too short and scary, especially at unfamiliar stations.

Four redesign priorities

Across the two sessions, confidence depended on more than travel time: participants needed to understand the settings, judge the connections and know what to do when the route changed. The next step is to test these directions in a prototype and check whether travellers can spot a risky connection, correct their search and continue after a delay.
